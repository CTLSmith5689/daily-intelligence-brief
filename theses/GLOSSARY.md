# Glossary

The definitions every investment memo uses. A memo defines each term in one plain sentence where
it first uses it, and ends with a glossary of the terms it used. Both copy their wording from this
file word for word, so a term means the same thing in every memo and a reader who has learned it
once does not have to learn it again.

The analyst does not edit this file. When a memo needs a term that is not here, the analyst defines
it in one plain sentence in the same style and lists it in the run manifest as a proposed addition.
`theses/bin/validate.py` warns when a memo's glossary gives a term a definition different from the
one below, or uses a term that is not here.

Terms are in alphabetical order. An abbreviation follows its term in brackets, and a memo may use
the abbreviation after the first use.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| 10-K | The annual report a US company files with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). |
| 10-Q | The quarterly report a US company files with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). |
| 52-week range | The lowest and highest closing prices of the past year. |
| Accounts receivable | Money customers owe for goods already delivered. |
| Accruals | Profit a company has booked but not yet received as cash. |
| Action | The step a memo asks the portfolio manager to take: Initiate, Add, Hold, Trim, Exit, Avoid or Short. |
| Add | Buying more of a stock the portfolio already owns. |
| Average daily volume | The number of shares traded on an average day, here over the past three months. |
| Avoid | Owning none of a stock. |
| Balance sheet | A statement of what a company owns and what it owes on a given date. |
| Base case | The middle outcome the analyst thinks plausible, built from stated assumptions. |
| Basis point | One hundredth of a percentage point. |
| Bear case | The bad outcome the analyst thinks plausible, built from stated assumptions. |
| Beta | How much a stock tends to move for each 1% move in the market. |
| Blume adjustment | Moving a measured beta one third of the way toward 1.0, because betas tend to drift there. |
| Book value | What a company owns minus what it owes, as its balance sheet records them. |
| Bull case | The good outcome the analyst thinks plausible, built from stated assumptions. |
| Buyback | The company buying its own shares, which reduces the share count. |
| Capital asset pricing model (CAPM) | A formula that sets the required return at the risk-free rate plus beta times the equity risk premium. |
| Capital expenditure (capex) | Spending on equipment and buildings. |
| Cash conversion | The share of profit that arrives as cash from operations. |
| Catalyst | A dated event that should show whether the thesis is right. |
| Comparable companies (comps) | Listed businesses similar enough that their valuation ratios give a reference point. |
| Consensus estimates | The average of other analysts' forecasts. |
| Cost of equity | The required return for shareholders. |
| Cost of revenue | The cost of the goods or services a company sold. |
| Cyclical | Describes a business whose profits rise and fall with its industry's spending rather than growing steadily. |
| Days sales outstanding (DSO) | Receivables divided by a quarter's revenue, times 91 days. |
| Diluted shares | The share count including options and awards that would become shares. |
| Dilution | New shares that shrink each existing share's part of the company. |
| Discount rate | The annual return an investor requires for the risk, used to reduce future cash to today's money. |
| Discounted cash flow (DCF) | A model that values a company as the sum of its future free cash flows, each reduced to today's money. |
| Dividend | Cash paid to shareholders for each share. |
| Dividend yield | A year's dividends as a share of the price. |
| Downside | How far the price could fall. |
| Drawdown | The largest fall in the price from a high point to a later low. |
| Earnings per share (EPS) | The profit attributable to each share. |
| Earnings release (Form 8-K) | The press release, filed with the SEC on Form 8-K, in which a company announces its quarterly results. |
| EBITDA | Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation. |
| Enterprise value (EV) | Market capitalisation plus debt minus cash, roughly the price of the whole business free of its financing. |
| Equity risk premium | The extra annual return required for owning shares instead of government debt. |
| Estimate (E) | A forecast figure, marked E after the year, as in FY2027E. |
| EV/EBITDA | Enterprise value divided by EBITDA. |
| EV/revenue | Enterprise value divided by revenue, useful when earnings are small or negative. |
| Exit | Selling all of a holding. |
| Exit rule | A condition, stated before buying, under which the analyst would sell or cut a holding. |
| Expected return | The percentage gain from the last close to the probability-weighted value, plus any dividends expected over the same 12 months. |
| Fabless | Designing chips but paying outside foundries and contract manufacturers to make them. |
| Falsifier | A specific, dated observation that would show a view is wrong. |
| FCF yield | Free cash flow divided by market capitalisation. |
| Fiscal year (FY) | The twelve months a company's accounts cover. |
| Forward P/E | Price divided by forecast earnings per share. |
| Free cash flow (FCF) | The cash left after running the business and paying for equipment. |
| Free cash flow margin | Free cash flow as a share of revenue. |
| GAAP | The US accounting rules that a company's reported results follow. |
| Graphics processing unit (GPU) | A processor that runs many calculations in parallel. |
| Gross margin | Revenue minus the cost of revenue, as a share of revenue. |
| Guidance | Management's own published forecast. |
| Hold | Keeping a holding at its current size. |
| Inference | Running a trained artificial intelligence model to produce answers. |
| Initiate | Buying a first holding in a stock. |
| Inventory write-down | A charge for goods that can no longer be sold at cost. |
| Investment grade | A credit rating that marks a borrower as likely to repay. |
| Investment memo | The note in which an analyst asks the portfolio manager to act. |
| Investment thesis | The short argument for why a stock is mispriced, or why it is not. |
| Long | Owning shares, which profits if the price rises. |
| Management's discussion and analysis (MD&A) | The section of a 10-Q or 10-K where management explains the results. |
| Market capitalisation | The share price times the number of shares, the market value of all the equity. |
| Mid-year convention | Treating each year's cash as arriving in the middle of that year. |
| Momentum | A price that has been rising, or falling, for months. |
| Monitoring plan | The figures the analyst checks each quarter, with the threshold that would change the recommendation. |
| n/a | Not available. |
| n/m | Not meaningful, as for a P/E when earnings are negative. |
| Net cash | Cash minus debt. |
| Net debt | Debt minus cash. |
| Net margin | Net income as a share of revenue. |
| Next twelve months (NTM) | The four quarters following a given date. |
| Non-GAAP | The company's adjusted version of its results, which leaves out items it considers unusual. |
| Operating expenses | The costs of running the business beyond making the product, such as research, selling and administration. |
| Operating income | The profit left after the cost of revenue and operating expenses. |
| Operating margin | Operating income as a share of revenue. |
| Other income | Income from outside operations, such as interest and gains or losses on investments. |
| P/E | Price divided by earnings per share. |
| Percentage point | The unit of difference between two percentages. |
| Portfolio manager (PM) | The person who decides what the portfolio owns and in what size. |
| Position size | A holding's value as a share of the portfolio's value. |
| Pre-mortem | Imagining that a call has already failed and asking why. |
| Price target | The analyst's estimate of what the shares will be worth in 12 months. |
| Priced in | The growth and margins that today's price needs in order to be a fair price. |
| Probability-weighted value | Each case's value multiplied by its probability, with the results added. |
| Required return | The annual return a stock must offer to pay for its risk. |
| Return on equity (ROE) | Net income as a share of the money shareholders have put in and left in the company. |
| Revenue concentration | The share of sales that comes from a few buyers. |
| Reverse DCF | Working backwards from the price to the growth it requires. |
| Risk budget | A holding's size times its volatility. |
| Risk-free rate | The return on US government debt. |
| Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) | The US agency that regulates public companies and receives their filings. |
| Segment | A part of the business for which the company reports revenue and profit separately. |
| Sensitivity table | A table showing how a result changes when two assumptions change together. |
| Sequential growth | Growth over the previous quarter. |
| Short | Selling borrowed shares, a trade that profits if the price falls. |
| Stock split | Giving each shareholder more shares, each worth proportionately less, with no change in the company's value. |
| Stock-based compensation | The cost of paying employees in shares. |
| Supply commitments | Contracts to buy parts and manufacturing capacity in the future. |
| Terminal value | A figure standing in for every year after the forecast, assuming cash flow grows at a constant rate forever. |
| Total return | The change in price plus dividends received, as a share of the starting price. |
| Trailing twelve months (TTM) | The four most recently reported quarters. |
| Training | Building an artificial intelligence model from data. |
| Trim | Selling part of a holding. |
| Unrealised gain | An increase in the value of a holding that has not been sold. |
| Upside | How far the price could rise. |
| Valuation multiple | A price divided by a measure of profit or size. |
| Variant perception | Where the analyst's forecast differs from what the price requires, with a checkable reason the price has not yet moved. |
| Volatility | How widely a stock's daily price changes vary, stated as a yearly figure. |
| Weighted average cost of capital (WACC) | The cost of debt and the cost of equity blended by their weights in the company's capital. |
| Working capital | Money tied up in receivables and inventory, net of what the company owes its suppliers. |
| Year over year (YoY) | Compared with the same period a year earlier. |
