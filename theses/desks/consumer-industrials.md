# Consumer and industrials

Covers Consumer Discretionary, Consumer Staples and Industrials. Its playbooks are `sectors/consumer-discretionary.md`, `sectors/consumer-staples.md` and `sectors/industrials.md`.

## What this desk cares about

These are the sectors that move with the economy, and the desk's first question is where each company sits in its own cycle. A memo on a car maker, a homebuilder, a machinery maker or a trucking company says which year of the reported history its bear case resembles, because a low multiple at the top of a cycle is the most common mistake here.

The second question is whether growth is real. For retailers and restaurants that means comparable sales, not new stores. For staples it means volume, not price alone. For industrials it means orders and backlog, not a single strong quarter.

The desk also watches working capital. Inventory that grows faster than sales, or receivables that swell in a boom, absorb cash that the income statement counts as profit.

None of those figures are on the panel: comparable sales, inventory, volume, orders and backlog come only from the filing text, and the memo says which filing.
