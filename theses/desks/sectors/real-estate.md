# Real Estate

Real estate investment trusts (REITs) that own shops, offices, warehouses, flats and houses, hospitals and care homes, hotels, data centres, towers and storage, plus real estate services and developers. Owned by the Financials and real estate desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. What does the property earn, and is that rising? Net operating income from the same properties year on year, occupancy and the rent on new leases against expiring ones.
2. What is the property worth against the share price? A REIT's shares can trade well above or below the value of the buildings it owns.
3. How much debt, and when is it due? REITs pay out most of their income, so refinancing at a higher rate comes straight out of what is left for shareholders.
4. What kind of tenant? A warehouse leased for ten years to a large company is a different risk from a hotel room sold nightly.

## How to value it

- **REITs.** Price to adjusted funds from operations (P/AFFO). Funds from operations (FFO) adds depreciation back to net income and removes gains on property sales; AFFO also subtracts the spending needed to keep the buildings let. Cross-check with the value of the properties (net asset value) where the filing supports it, and with the dividend against AFFO.
- **Real estate services and brokers.** P/E and EV/EBITDA, with transaction volumes as the driver, closer to a financial company.
- **Developers and homebuilders listed here.** Price to book, with the value of land held and the margin on sales.

## Traps

- Valuing a REIT on P/E. Depreciation on buildings is large, and buildings often hold their value, so net income understates what the business earns.
- Reading a high dividend yield as cheap when AFFO does not cover it.
- Treating the sector as one market. Office and data centre REITs can move in opposite directions in the same year.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 110 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- The median P/E is 27. For the 91 with positive net income, market value divided by net income plus depreciation has a median of 12.9, against 26.8 on net income alone. A P/E comparison with other sectors is meaningless here.
- The panel has no FFO, AFFO, occupancy or net operating income field. Net income plus depreciation is a rough stand-in for FFO, but it keeps gains on property sales in and is not AFFO.
- Only 54 of the 110 have a free cash flow figure, so a free cash flow yield ranks half the sector on nothing.
- The vendor EV/EBITDA differs from the screen's own recomputation by 20% at the median, the widest gap of any sector.
- Median net debt is 5.5 times EBITDA, normal for a REIT and high anywhere else.
