# Financials

Banks, insurers, asset managers, brokers, exchanges, payment processors and consumer lenders. Owned by the Financials and real estate desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. What return does it earn on its capital, and can it last? For a bank or insurer, return on equity against the cost of equity decides whether the shares deserve to trade above book value.
2. What could the losses be? A lender's bad year is decided by the credit losses it has not yet taken; an insurer's by claims it has not yet reserved for.
3. How is it funded? Deposits that can leave quickly, or funding that reprices faster than the loans, turn a rate change into a loss.
4. How much capital does it hold above the regulator's minimum? That surplus decides buybacks and dividends.

## How to value it

- **Banks.** Price to tangible book value, against return on tangible common equity (ROTCE). A bank earning well above its cost of equity deserves a premium to tangible book. Watch net interest margin, loan growth, credit losses and deposit costs.
- **Insurers.** Price to book against return on equity. For property and casualty insurers, add the combined ratio (claims and expenses as a share of premiums; below 100% is an underwriting profit) and reserve development (whether past claims turned out worse or better than reserved).
- **Asset managers, brokers and exchanges.** P/E, with client money flows and fee rates.
- **Payment processors.** P/E and free cash flow, with payment volume growth, as for a software company.

## Traps

- Using EV/EBITDA or net debt for a bank. Debt is the raw material of a bank, not a financing choice.
- Reading a low price to book as cheap when the return on equity is below the cost of equity.
- Treating a quarter's reserve release as recurring profit.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 329 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- Of the 127 banks, only 2 have an EBITDA figure and 1 an EV/EBITDA, and gross margin is 0 for 126 of them. EV/revenue is filled in for 125, but a bank's revenue is not comparable with an industrial company's.
- Only 2 of the 127 banks get an overall screen score, because too few of their quality figures exist. The screen almost never hands a bank to the analyst, so bank coverage has to be assigned.
- Price to book here is on total book value, including goodwill. The panel has no goodwill or intangible asset field, so price to tangible book and ROTCE must be worked out from the balance sheet in the filing.
- Of the 66 insurers, 15 have EBITDA but 58 carry a vendor EV/EBITDA. The screen uses only its own recomputation, so the vendor figure should not be quoted.
- The panel has no net interest margin, deposits, capital ratio, credit loss, combined ratio or reserve development field.
- Banks and insurers are split across duplicate labels: "Regional Banks" (83) and "Banks - Regional" (36), "Property & Casualty Insurance" (23) and "Insurance - Property & Casualty" (12).
