# Information Technology

Software, semiconductors and the equipment that makes them, computer and network hardware, electronic components, and IT services. Owned by the Technology and communications desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. Where is the company in its cycle? Chip makers and hardware sellers go through booms and gluts. A high margin can be the top of a cycle rather than a new normal.
2. How fast is revenue growing, and is the growth paying for itself? Growth that needs ever more spending on sales is worth less than growth that arrives with rising margins.
3. How concentrated is the business? One customer, one product or one country can decide a year.
4. What does the price require? Many of these shares cost 40 times earnings or more, so the argument usually turns on how long fast growth has to last.

## How to value it

- **Software.** Revenue growth and operating margin together, and free cash flow once it is positive. EV/revenue only where earnings are small or negative. Say how much of the free cash flow comes from counting stock-based pay as if it cost nothing.
- **Semiconductors and chip equipment.** P/E or EV/EBITDA on earnings across a whole cycle, not the latest 12 months. Compare the current gross margin with the company's own worst and typical years in the reported history.
- **Hardware and components.** P/E and free cash flow yield. Watch gross margin, because component costs pass through to prices with a lag.
- **IT services.** P/E and free cash flow, with margin as clients cut or shift spending.

## Traps

- Calling a chip maker cheap on peak earnings. A P/E of 15 at the top of a cycle can be a P/E of 40 a year later with the price unchanged.
- Reading revenue growth that came from an acquisition as growth the business earned.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 299 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- 91 of the 299 have no usable P/E, mostly because they lost money over the past year. A ranking on P/E silently drops them.
- The panel has no field for annual recurring revenue, remaining performance obligations, backlog or bookings, so the figures that drive software and equipment valuations exist only in the filing text.
- The panel has no stock-based pay field. Free cash flow here is after adding back stock-based pay, as the cash flow statement does, so it flatters companies that pay staff in shares.
- Software is split across two labels for the same thing, "Application Software" (39 names) and "Software - Application" (38). A peer group built on one label misses half the peers.
- Among the 67 chip makers and chip equipment makers, the median P/E is 48, and 16 had a negative operating margin. The sector median says little about any one of them.
