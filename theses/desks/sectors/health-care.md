# Health Care

Drug makers, biotechnology companies, medical device and equipment makers, health insurers, hospitals and care providers, distributors, and makers of research tools. Owned by the Health care desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. What happens when the main patents expire? For a drug maker, the date its best sellers lose protection often decides the next decade.
2. What is in development, and at what stage? A drug in the last stage of trials is worth far more than one in the first, and most early drugs fail.
3. How long does the cash last? A biotech company that loses money needs to raise more before its trials read out, and raising it dilutes holders.
4. Who pays, and at what price? Government programmes, insurers and pharmacy benefit managers set prices, and a change in their rules can move revenue quickly.
5. For insurers and providers: what share of premiums goes on care (the medical loss ratio), and is it rising?

## How to value it

- **Large drug makers.** P/E and free cash flow, with a schedule of patent expiries against the drugs that will replace the lost sales.
- **Biotechnology.** Cash runway (cash divided by the yearly cash burn) and the value of the pipeline, drug by drug, weighted by the chance each one succeeds. EV/revenue only where there is revenue. P/E is meaningless for most of them.
- **Devices and equipment.** P/E and EV/EBITDA, with procedure volumes and new product launches as the drivers.
- **Health insurers.** P/E, with the medical loss ratio and enrolment.
- **Providers and distributors.** EV/EBITDA and free cash flow on thin margins, with volumes and reimbursement rates.

## Traps

- Treating a trial result or approval date as certain. Say the probability and where it comes from.
- Valuing a biotech on sales forecasts without the cost and time of getting there.
- Reading a drug maker's low P/E as cheap without checking when its main drug loses patent protection.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23.

- 61 health care companies worth more than $1 billion have no revenue on the panel, 52 of them biotechnology. The screen requires revenue, so it never hands one of them to the analyst.
- Of the 285 that pass the screen's gate, only 143 have a usable P/E, and 91 had a negative operating margin. 50 carry a negative vendor EV/EBITDA, which the screen discards.
- Of the 99 biotechnology companies that pass the gate, 71 lost money. The 66 burning cash hold a median of 1.85 years of cash at their trailing free cash flow. Cash and investments divided by that burn is a usable runway figure.
- The panel has no field for pipeline stage, trial dates, patent expiries or medical loss ratio. Those come only from the filing text.
