# Communication Services

Telephone, wireless and cable networks, internet platforms and search, advertising, publishing, broadcasting, film, streaming and video games. Owned by the Technology and communications desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. What does a customer pay, and is that going up or down? For a network it is revenue per subscriber; for a platform it is revenue per user or per advertisement shown.
2. How much has to be spent to keep the business where it is? Networks need constant capital spending, and content businesses need constant spending on new shows and games.
3. How much debt does it carry against its cash flow? Telecom and cable companies run with high debt, so a small fall in cash flow matters a lot to the shares.
4. For advertising businesses: how much of revenue moves with the economy?

## How to value it

- **Telecom, wireless and cable.** EV/EBITDA and free cash flow yield, because debt is a large part of the capital. Check free cash flow after capital spending, and whether the dividend is covered by it.
- **Internet platforms and search.** P/E and free cash flow, with revenue growth and operating margin. Where a company runs several businesses, say which one the value depends on.
- **Media, film and streaming.** EV/EBITDA, with care: spending on content is often capitalised and then amortised, so EBITDA can look healthy while cash goes out.
- **Video games.** P/E and free cash flow, noting how dependent the year is on one or two releases and their dates.
- **Advertising agencies and publishers.** P/E and free cash flow yield, set against a revenue line that shrinks when clients cut budgets.

## Traps

- Reading a low EV/EBITDA at a cable or telecom company as cheap when capital spending uses up most of the EBITDA.
- Treating subscriber growth as good news without the price each subscriber pays.
- Valuing a platform on its user count rather than on what it earns from each user.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 79 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- Only 43 of the 79 have a usable P/E, and 25 had a negative operating margin over the past year. A sector median P/E describes the profitable minority.
- Among the 20 telecom, wireless and cable companies, the median net debt is 5.9 times EBITDA. Debt measures from other sectors do not transfer.
- The panel has no subscriber, revenue per user, churn or content-spending field. Those figures exist only in the filing text.
- Entertainment is split across two labels, "Movies & Entertainment" (9 names) and "Entertainment" (9), so a peer group built on one misses the other.
