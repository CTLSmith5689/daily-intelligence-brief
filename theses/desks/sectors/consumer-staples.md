# Consumer Staples

Packaged food and drink makers, household and personal care products, tobacco, brewers and distillers, grocery stores and warehouse clubs, food distributors and farm products. Owned by the Consumer and industrials desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. Is revenue growing from selling more, or only from charging more? Price rises without volume growth run out when shoppers switch to cheaper brands or store brands.
2. Can the company pass on its costs? Ingredient, packaging and freight costs move faster than shelf prices, so gross margin shows who has pricing power.
3. How much of the growth comes from acquisitions? Many of these companies grow by buying brands, and pay for them with debt.
4. For grocers and distributors: margins are thin, so small changes in cost or price decide the year.

## How to value it

- **Branded food, drink and household products.** P/E and EV/EBITDA against organic growth (growth excluding acquisitions and currency) and gross margin. Free cash flow yield against the dividend.
- **Tobacco.** Free cash flow yield and dividend cover, with volume declines against price rises.
- **Grocers, clubs and distributors.** P/E and EV/EBITDA, with sales per square foot or comparable sales, and the operating margin.
- **Farm products and commodity processors.** EV/EBITDA on earnings across a cycle; these behave more like materials companies.

## Traps

- Reading a low P/E as cheap when the year's net income was cut by a write-down or restructuring charge, or as expensive when it was not.
- Treating price-led revenue growth as a healthy business.
- Using price to book for companies whose buybacks and acquisitions have hollowed out book equity.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 86 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- Revenue growth on the panel is one number. It cannot separate price from volume, currency or acquisitions; the filing text can.
- 5 have negative book equity and no price to book. 16 have no usable P/E.
- The panel has no volume, organic growth or comparable sales field.
- Packaged food is split across two labels, "Packaged Foods & Meats" (20 names) and "Packaged Foods" (5).
