# Consumer Discretionary

Retailers, restaurants, hotels, cruise lines and casinos, car makers and parts suppliers, homebuilders, clothing and luxury brands, leisure products and education. Owned by the Consumer and industrials desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. Are sales growing from the same stores, or only from new ones? Comparable sales (sales at stores or restaurants open more than a year) separate demand from expansion.
2. Is the stock of unsold goods under control? Inventory growing faster than sales usually ends in discounts that cut the margin.
3. How exposed is it to a slowdown in spending? Spending on cars, houses, travel and clothes falls first when households feel poorer.
4. What does the brand allow it to charge? Price rises that stick are the best evidence a brand has value; price rises that are reversed with discounts are not.
5. For homebuilders: orders, the margin on homes sold, and the land held for future building.

## How to value it

- **Retailers.** P/E and EV/EBITDA, with comparable sales, gross margin and inventory against sales. For a retailer that leases its stores, EBITDA leaves out the rent, so compare like with like.
- **Restaurants and hotels.** EV/EBITDA and free cash flow. Franchise-heavy companies earn fees and hold little capital, so they trade at higher multiples than those that own their sites.
- **Car makers and parts suppliers.** P/E and EV/EBITDA on earnings across a cycle, with volumes and pricing.
- **Homebuilders.** Price to book, against return on equity, with orders and gross margin on homes.
- **Brands and luxury.** P/E, with price increases against volume and the share of sales at full price.

## Traps

- A low P/E at a peak in the cycle, above all for car makers and homebuilders.
- Reading revenue growth from new stores as growth in demand.
- Using price to book for franchisors and asset-light brands, many of which have negative book value from buybacks.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 220 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- 24 have negative book equity, among them McDonald's, Starbucks, Hilton, Marriott, AutoZone and Lowe's, so 26 have no price to book at all. The Value score for them rests on the other measures.
- The panel has no comparable sales, inventory, store count or order field. Those come only from the filing text.
- 38 have no usable P/E.
