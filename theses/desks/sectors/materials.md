# Materials

Chemicals, fertilisers, steel, aluminium, copper, gold and other mining, construction materials, industrial gases, and packaging. Owned by the Energy, materials and utilities desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. Where is the price of what it sells against its own history? Most of these companies take the market price for their product, so profit moves with it.
2. Where does the company sit on the cost curve? The lowest-cost producer keeps earning when prices fall; the highest-cost one stops.
3. How much capital does it take to stay in business? Mines deplete and plants wear out, so depreciation is a real cost here.
4. For specialty chemicals and industrial gases: how much of revenue is under long contracts or sold on performance rather than on price?

## How to value it

- **Commodity producers (fertiliser, steel, aluminium, copper, commodity chemicals).** EV/EBITDA on earnings across a cycle, and free cash flow yield at a stated price. Compare the latest margin with the best, worst and typical year in the reported history.
- **Gold and other miners.** Cost to produce an ounce or a tonne, the life of the mine, and EV/EBITDA at a stated metal price.
- **Specialty chemicals and industrial gases.** P/E and EV/EBITDA, closer to how an industrial company is valued, with volume growth and margin as the drivers.
- **Construction materials and packaging.** EV/EBITDA and free cash flow, with price increases against volume.

## Traps

- A low P/E at a peak margin. CF Industries had a P/E of 9 with an operating margin of 41% on the 2026-09-23 panel. That is a cheap multiple on a margin its own history shows is not typical.
- Treating a year of high product prices as the new level.
- Ignoring the cost of replacing reserves or rebuilding plants when judging free cash flow.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 93 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- There is no commodity price series, and no field for volumes, cost per unit or reserves. Those come only from the filing text.
- 14 of the 93 had a negative operating margin, and 28 have no usable P/E. Mosaic, for example, has no P/E because it lost money, while its neighbour CF prints 9.
- The screen scores a figure against the company's sub-industry only when at least 20 of its members report that figure, and otherwise against the whole sector. Only specialty chemicals (27 names) is that large, so a copper miner (3 in its group) or a gas maker (2) is scored against a sector where specialty chemicals makers are the biggest group.
