# Industrials

Aerospace and defence, machinery, building products, electrical equipment, construction and engineering, railways, trucking and shipping, airlines, distributors, and business services. Owned by the Consumer and industrials desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. Where is the order book going? Orders and backlog lead revenue by months or years for machinery, aerospace and engineering companies.
2. How much of revenue comes back after the sale? Spare parts and service are steadier and more profitable than new equipment.
3. How cyclical is demand? Construction, freight and industrial production swing with the economy, and costs do not fall as fast as revenue.
4. How good is the company at turning profit into cash? Working capital (stock, receivables) swells in a boom and can absorb much of the profit.
5. For airlines and transport: fuel, labour costs and capacity in the industry.

## How to value it

- **Machinery, electrical and building products.** P/E and EV/EBITDA on earnings across a cycle, with orders, backlog and margin.
- **Aerospace and defence.** P/E and free cash flow, with backlog, the delivery schedule and the share of revenue from aftermarket parts and service.
- **Construction and engineering.** P/E with backlog, and margin on fixed-price contracts, where cost overruns fall on the company.
- **Railways, trucking and shipping.** EV/EBITDA, with volumes, pricing and operating ratio (costs as a share of revenue).
- **Airlines.** EV/EBITDA including leases, with unit revenue against unit cost.
- **Business services and distributors.** P/E and free cash flow, with organic growth.

## Traps

- A low multiple at the top of the cycle. Machinery and transport earnings can halve in a downturn.
- Treating a large backlog as profit already earned: fixed-price contracts can lose money.
- Reading a defence contract award as revenue for this year when it is spread over many.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 331 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- The panel has no order, backlog, book-to-bill or delivery field. Those come only from the filing text.
- 72 have no usable P/E, and 37 had a negative operating margin.
- The 9 airlines had a median operating margin of 3.5%, and 3 were negative. Airline figures say little about the rest of the sector.
- Machinery carries overlapping labels, "Industrial Machinery & Supplies & Components" (42 names) and "Specialty Industrial Machinery" (8), from two different classification schemes.
