# Energy

Oil and gas producers, refiners, pipelines and storage, drillers and oilfield services, coal and uranium. Owned by the Energy, materials and utilities desk.

## The questions that decide a stock

1. What oil or gas price does the company need? A producer's profit is mostly the commodity price less its cost to get a barrel out of the ground.
2. How long do its reserves last, and what does it cost to replace them? A producer that is not replacing what it pumps is slowly selling itself.
3. What does it do with the cash? In a good year the cash goes to debt, dividends, buybacks or new wells, and the choice says a lot about the next bad year.
4. For refiners: what is the margin between crude and the fuels made from it, and where is that margin against its own history?
5. For pipelines: how much of revenue is fixed fees under long contracts, and how much moves with volumes or prices?

## How to value it

- **Producers (exploration and production).** EV/EBITDA and free cash flow yield at a stated commodity price, cross-checked against the value of reserves where the 10-K gives it. State the breakeven price the argument assumes.
- **Refiners.** EV/EBITDA and P/E on earnings across a cycle, not a peak year. Compare the latest margin with the best, worst and typical year in the reported history.
- **Pipelines and storage.** EV/EBITDA and dividend cover by distributable cash flow, with net debt against EBITDA.
- **Drillers and services.** EV/EBITDA on cycle-average earnings, with the rig count or activity level as the driver.

## Traps

- A low P/E at the top of the commodity cycle. The earnings that make it look cheap are the ones most likely to fall.
- Treating a production target as a forecast of profit, when the price of the commodity decides the profit.
- Reading a big dividend as safe without checking it against free cash flow at a lower price.

## Where our data misleads

Checked against the panel of 2026-09-23, on the 118 companies in this sector that pass the screen's size and revenue gate.

- There is no commodity price series anywhere in the pipeline, and no field for reserves, production volumes or breakeven prices. Those come only from the filing text; say so when an argument depends on them.
- The screen recomputes EV/EBITDA from the panel's own debt, cash and EBITDA, and can do so for only 78 of the 118. Where it can, the vendor figure differs from the recomputed one by 11% at the median.
- 22 of the 118 have a negative free cash flow yield, so a sector median yield mixes companies spending heavily on new wells with those returning cash.
- Producers carry two labels, "Oil & Gas Exploration & Production" (24 names) and "Oil & Gas E&P" (6), so a peer group on one label misses the other.
