## WHAT THE COMPANY DOES

NVIDIA designs the chips and the computers that train and run artificial intelligence programs. It sells them mostly as finished racks of equipment, along with the networking that joins hundreds of thousands of chips into one machine and the software that runs on top. Its buyers are cloud companies, the laboratories that build artificial intelligence models, businesses, startups and governments. It also sells graphics chips for games and for design work.

The company reports itself in two parts. Compute and Networking sold $88 billion of the $96 billion NVIDIA sold in the three months to July 2026. Graphics, which is games and workstation chips, sold $8 billion. Buyers with head offices in the United States accounted for $60 billion of that quarter, and the rest went to buyers in Taiwan, China and elsewhere.

## HOW IT MAKES MONEY

Three things decide whether the profit rises or falls. They are how many systems NVIDIA ships, what is left of each $1 of sales after the cost of building them, and how fast its own running costs grow.

In the three months to July 2026 NVIDIA sold $96 billion, up 106 percent from a year earlier. Of each $1 of sales, 75 cents was left after the cost of making the product, against 72 cents a year earlier. Management says the improvement came from selling more of its newest systems. Running costs were $8 billion, up 55 percent from a year earlier. What was left was an operating profit of $64 billion.

The data centre business is almost all of it. That business sold $89 billion in the quarter, up 117 percent from a year earlier. The buyers are few. Over the six months to July, three direct customers were 16 percent, 15 percent and 13 percent of sales.

## THE LAST TEN YEARS

I have twelve years of filed figures, from the year to January 2015 to the year to January 2026. Sales went from $4.7 billion to $216 billion. The weakest stretch was the year to January 2023, when sales stopped growing at $27 billion and operating profit fell from $10 billion to $4.2 billion. Then the orders for artificial intelligence equipment arrived: sales of $61 billion, then $131 billion, then $216 billion in the three years to January 2026.

Profit per share cannot be compared across the whole run, because NVIDIA split its shares twice and the filed figures are not adjusted for it. For the three years that can be compared, profit per share was $1.19, then $2.94, then $4.90.

The filings say why profit can swing so far. NVIDIA commits to parts and manufacturing capacity long before it ships anything. In the six months to July 2025 it charged $6.3 billion against stock it could not sell and purchases it could not use. Export rules on chips bound for China caused most of that charge.

## WHAT MANAGEMENT DOES WITH THE CASH

In the six months to July 2026 NVIDIA's operations brought in $74 billion of cash and it spent $4.4 billion on equipment, leaving $70 billion of spare cash. It spent $40 billion buying back 203 million of its own shares and $6.3 billion on dividends. In May 2026 the board approved another $80 billion of share buying, leaving $99 billion approved and unspent. It also raised the quarterly dividend twenty-five fold.

NVIDIA owes $33 billion after issuing $25 billion of notes in June 2026. Against that it holds $57 billion of cash and short-term investments and $43 billion of shares in other companies.

Its promises are much larger than its debts. It has committed $279 billion to suppliers for parts and capacity, against $119 billion three months earlier. It holds $99 billion of investments in other companies. It has guaranteed up to $105 billion of rent and power payments on data centres that OpenAI will lease from 2029. It has also promised to pay cloud companies for computing it may not use itself.

## WHAT HAS TO BE TRUE FOR THE PRICE TO MAKE SENSE

NVIDIA closed at $228.87. That is 29 times the $7.91 it earned for each share over the past 12 months.

The company has published its own forecast for the current quarter. It expects sales of $108 billion in the three months to October 2026. It expects 74 cents left of each $1 of sales after the cost of making the product, and running costs of $9.2 billion. On its own tax rate that comes to about $2.48 a share. Add the $4.85 already earned in the first half and a fourth quarter like the third, and the year to January 2027 earns about $10 a share. At $228.87 the price is 23 times that.

So the price does not need a surprise. It needs this year to land near the company's own forecast, and the year after to be larger again.

## WHERE I DISAGREE

I do not disagree with the price. I have no view worth betting on here, so I am watching instead, and what I want to watch is whether the profit turns into cash.

In the six months to July 2026 NVIDIA reported profit of $118 billion. Its operations produced $74 billion of cash over the same six months. Two changes account for most of the difference. Money owed by customers rose from $38 billion to $63 billion, and stock on hand rose from $21 billion to $32 billion. Management gives a reason for the first: it granted longer payment terms on large multi-quarter agreements to customers it describes as investment grade.

There is a second gap inside the profit. NVIDIA made $141 billion before tax in those six months, and $24 billion of that came from other income rather than from selling equipment. Most of that is the changing value of shares it holds in other companies.

Neither fact makes the price wrong, and I am not claiming it is. Both make reported profit a weaker guide to cash than it was a year ago, at a company that is also guaranteeing its customers' rent and buying shares in them.

## WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT

NVIDIA reports the quarter ending in October 2026 in late November 2026, and the year ending in January 2027 in February 2027. Each report carries the cash flow statement and the money owed by customers. Two of them are enough to tell one slow quarter from a pattern.

## WHAT THE SHARES COULD BE WORTH

Over the past year NVIDIA's shares have cost between $164.79 and $235.20. Against the $4.90 it earned for each share in the year to January 2026, that is 34 to 48 times. I use lower figures than that below, because the profit I am applying them to is this year's and next year's rather than last year's.

- Bad case, $123. A year like the one to January 2023, when sales stopped growing and operating profit fell by more than half. Profit per share back at $4.90, the figure for the year to January 2026, and the stock costing 25 times it.
- Middle case, $240. About $10 a share in the year to January 2027, which is the company's own forecast for this quarter carried through the year, and the stock costing 24 times it.
- Good case, $364. $13 a share in the year to January 2028, and the stock costing 28 times it.

I will be scored on $240, about 5 percent above the latest close, over about 8 months.

## WHAT WOULD PROVE ME WRONG

One result would prove me wrong. If NVIDIA's cash from operations in the six months to January 2027 comes within 10 percent of the profit it reports for those months, the gap was timing. Waiting would have been the wrong call.

The strongest argument against me is that this is what fast growth looks like. A company whose sales doubled in a year has to build stock before it ships it, and large customers pay on terms. NVIDIA still collected $70 billion of spare cash in the half and handed $46 billion of it back to shareholders, so the waiting costs it nothing.

My view survives that argument, for one reason. The test above costs me two quarters and settles the question with the company's own figures. NVIDIA has promised $279 billion to suppliers and up to $105 billion of guarantees on one customer's data centres. Those promises are large enough that I would rather see the cash arrive first.

## WHAT I DON'T KNOW

- I cannot see which customers owe the $63 billion, or when each of them has to pay.
- I cannot see the orders NVIDIA has taken but not yet shipped.
- I do not know what NVIDIA charges for each system or how many it ships.
- I cannot tell how much of the $99 billion it has invested in other companies is invested in its own customers.
- The stored sales figure for NVIDIA is 40 percent away from what it reported, so every reading built on stored sales is unusable here.
