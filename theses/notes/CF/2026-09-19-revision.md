## WHAT THE COMPANY DOES

CF Industries turns natural gas into ammonia and sells it as nitrogen fertiliser. Its buyers are farm cooperatives, retailers, fertiliser distributors, wholesalers and industrial users. CF sells ammonia as it is, and upgrades the rest into granular urea, urea ammonium nitrate and ammonium nitrate. Ammonium nitrate also goes to the commercial explosives industry. Diesel exhaust fluid, urea liquor and nitric acid go to industrial buyers.

CF runs nine plants, in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom. Its Donaldsonville plant in Louisiana is the largest ammonia complex in the world. In 2025 CF sold 19.1 million tons of product for $7.08 billion.

## HOW IT MAKES MONEY

Three things decide whether CF's profit rises or falls. They are the price it gets for a ton of product, the number of tons it sells, and the cost of natural gas. Gas was about 36 percent of CF's production cost in the first half of 2026.

In the three months to June 2026 CF got $523 for the average ton, against $376 a year earlier. It sold 4.3 million tons, down from 5.0 million. Its gas cost $3.37 for each unit, against $3.36 a year earlier. Of those three, price was the only one that helped.

Granular urea earns more than any other product line. In the first half of 2026 it brought in $732 million after the cost of making it. Ammonium nitrate lost $15 million over the same six months, because the Yazoo City plant has been idle since an accident in November 2025.

## THE LAST TEN YEARS

CF's sales ran from $3.7 billion in 2016 to $11.2 billion in 2022, and were $7.1 billion in 2025. Profit per share moved much further. The best year was 2022 at $16.38 a share. The worst was 2016, a loss of $1.19. The typical year in CF's reported history earned $3.60. The latest full year, 2025, earned $8.97. Only 2022 was higher.

The filings say why profit swings so far. CF does not set its own prices. World supply and demand set them. CF's annual report gives an example. In the two years to the end of 2017 new plants opened around the world. The average price CF got for a ton fell 34 percent over those two years, from $314 to $207. The cost of running the plants changes little, so most of a change in price becomes a change in profit.

## WHAT MANAGEMENT DOES WITH THE CASH

Over the 12 months to June 2026 CF took in $2,977 million from its operations and spent $1,067 million on equipment and construction. After money paid to and received from its partners in a new plant, $1,823 million of spare cash was left. Management plans to spend about $950 million of CF's own money on equipment and construction in 2026.

Most of the spare cash has gone to shareholders. CF returned $1.3 billion over those 12 months in dividends and by buying back its own shares. It raised its quarterly dividend by 20 percent in July 2026. Its share count fell from 256.7 million in 2014 to 162.2 million in 2025, a drop of 37 percent. The buying has slowed. CF spent $245 million on its own shares in the first half of 2026, against $636 million in the first half of 2025.

The rest is going into a new plant. CF owns 40 percent of the Blue Point venture in Louisiana, which management estimates will cost about $3.7 billion. CF is separately spending about $550 million on storage and loading there. Construction started in August 2026 and production is expected to begin in 2029. CF can pay for this. It owes $3,250 million in bonds, none of it due before 2034, and it held $2,480 million of cash at the end of June 2026. Debt less cash is $770 million, which about three months of a normal year's earnings would clear.

## WHAT HAS TO BE TRUE FOR THE PRICE TO MAKE SENSE

CF's shares closed at $127.70 on 18 September 2026, up 53 percent over the past year. That is about 9.5 times the $13.46 a share CF earned over the past 12 months, which looks low.

The past 12 months were not a normal year. Management says a shortage caused by the conflict with Iran pushed world nitrogen prices up in March 2026. The average price CF got for a ton then rose 39 percent, to $523 in the three months to June. Two one-off gains helped as well: $170 million from settling a lawsuit and $75 million from insurance. After tax, and after a $23 million write-down at Yazoo City, those added about $1.09 a share.

Management publishes its own figure for what CF earns in a normal year. It is about $2.9 billion before interest, tax and the wearing out of its plants, close to what CF reported for 2025. CF earned $8.97 a share in 2025 and has fewer shares now, so the same profit today would be about $9.45 a share. At $127.70 the price is about 14 times that. The price makes sense only if a normal year for CF is now well above management's own figure.

## WHERE I DISAGREE

The note on CF written on 12 September 2026 made this claim:

> CF Industries makes nitrogen fertiliser, and its shares sit near a one-year high. At about 10 times
> last year's profit per share, the price only makes sense if those profits do not last. I would own
> it for a rise to $152 in about 8 months, because I think CF's almost-zero debt and spare cash leave
> its shares less room to fall.

I am REPLACING that claim. It was written without any of CF's own filings. The low debt in it is real and I keep that fact. I drop the conclusion, because that target price is 16 times what CF earns in a normal year.

I think CF's shares are priced on a profit figure that management has already said is coming down. In its August 2026 report, management gives its reasons why world nitrogen prices fell near the end of June 2026. The fertiliser application season ended. Buyers of ammonia for phosphate production cut back. Supply from the Middle East looked likely to return as a ceasefire was discussed.

The price has not moved on that, because no reported quarter contains it yet. CF's two reported quarters of 2026 both show profit roughly doubling. CF earned $8.71 a share in the six months to June, against $4.20 a year earlier. The fall management describes began in the last weeks of June, so the first quarter that will show it has not been filed.

## WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT

CF's report for the quarter ending 30 September 2026 is the first that will cover the months after prices fell back. CF publishes the average price it got for a ton in every quarterly report. It filed the June quarter's report on 6 August 2026, about five weeks after that quarter ended. So the September quarter's figure should be public before the end of 2026.

## WHAT THE SHARES COULD BE WORTH

At the end of each of the last four years, CF's shares cost between 5 and 13 times what it had earned that year. They cost 5 times at the end of 2022 and 10 times at the end of 2023. They cost 13 times at the end of 2024 and 9 times at the end of 2025. I use 11 times in all three cases below, near the top of that range, so a number I picked does not drive the answers. Each case takes its profit from CF's own record and adjusts it to the 153.9 million shares CF had in the June 2026 quarter.

- Bad case, $87. A year like 2024, when CF earned $6.74 a share, which at today's share count is $7.91. At 11 times that, the price is $87.
- Middle case, $104. A year like 2025, when CF earned $8.97 a share, which at today's share count is $9.45. That is also management's own figure for a normal year. At 11 times it, the price is $104.
- Good case, $148. The past 12 months repeat at $13.46 a share. At 11 times that, the price is $148.

I will be scored on $104, about 19 percent below the latest close, within about 8 months.

A second check points the same way. At the end of each of the last four years CF's spare cash was worth between $10.00 and $16.70 a year for every $100 of stock. Management's figure for spare cash in a normal year is more than $1.7 billion. At today's price that is about $8.80 for every $100 of stock. At $104 a share it would be about $10.80, back inside that range.

## WHAT WOULD PROVE ME WRONG

One result would prove me wrong. If the average price CF gets for a ton is $500 or more in the quarter ending 30 September 2026, the rise in prices has not unwound. CF got $523 a ton in the June quarter and $354 a ton in the first half of 2025, so $500 sits near the top of that range.

The strongest argument against me is the cash. Even in a normal year management expects more than $1.7 billion of spare cash. CF held $2,480 million of cash at the end of June 2026, and none of the $3,250 million it owes falls due before 2034. A company in that position can keep buying its own shares through a downturn, and its share count has already fallen 37 percent since 2014. Management also says supply stays tight into 2027, that European plants keep closing, and that producers in the Middle East will take time to return.

My view survives that argument for two reasons. CF has committed its 40 percent of a $3.7 billion plant plus $550 million of its own money, for production that starts in 2029. And it cut its share buying from $636 million to $245 million between the first halves of 2025 and 2026, while its cash balance rose. Neither point changes the test above, which is about the price of a ton of fertiliser.
